Roger Marshall’s voting record

A checkup on the doctor's voting record.

  • Voted for the Medicaid cuts

    July 1, 2025 · H.R. 1 · Senate roll call 372

    Marshall voted yes on the 2025 megabill, which passed 50-50 on the vice president's vote. The Congressional Budget Office projects about $1 trillion less federal spending on Medicaid and ACA marketplaces, plus 10 million more uninsured Americans by 2034.

    Sources: Senate roll call 372; American Hospital Association on the CBO estimate

  • Against a $35 insulin cap

    Aug. 7, 2022 · Amendment 5194 · Senate roll call 314

    A $35 monthly cap on insulin for people with private insurance won 57 votes but needed 60. Marshall voted no. The cap survived only for Medicare.

    Source: Senate roll call 314

  • An OB-GYN who blocked the IVF bill

    June 13, 2024 · S. 4445 · Senate roll call 197

    Marshall voted against even opening debate on the Right to IVF Act. The motion failed 48-47.

    Source: Senate roll call 197

  • Objected to the 2020 election, after the riot

    Jan. 6-7, 2021 · Senate roll calls 1 and 2

    Hours after the mob left the Capitol, Marshall voted to throw out Arizona's electors at 9:58 p.m. and Pennsylvania's after midnight. Both objections failed, 6-93 and 7-92.

    Sources: roll call 1; roll call 2

  • Said no to protecting rural hospitals

    June 30, 2025 · Markey motion on H.R. 1 · Senate roll call 333

    A motion to send the megabill back to committee with instructions on rural hospitals failed 49-51. Marshall, whose campaign says he kept the hospital's lights on, voted no.

    Sources: Senate roll call 333; Senate floor log, June 30, 2025

  • Let your premium help expire

    Dec. 11, 2025 · S. 3385 · Senate roll call 644

    A three-year extension of the ACA premium tax credits got 51 votes, short of the 60 it needed. Marshall voted no. The credits expired Dec. 31, 2025.

    Source: Senate roll call 644

  • Voted to repeal the ACA

    May 4, 2017 · H.R. 1628 · House roll call 256

    In the House, Marshall voted for the American Health Care Act, which passed 217-213. The Congressional Budget Office estimated it would leave 23 million more people uninsured by 2026.

    Sources: House roll call 256; CBO, May 24, 2017

  • Stalled care for burn-pit veterans

    July 27, 2022 · S. 3373 · Senate roll call 272

    The PACT Act covers veterans sickened by burn pits. Marshall voted to block it, and it stalled 55-42. He voted yes six days later, when it passed 86-11.

    Sources: roll call 272; roll call 280

  • Against the infrastructure law

    Aug. 10, 2021 · H.R. 3684 · Senate roll call 314

    The bill that pays for roads, bridges and rural broadband passed 69-30. Marshall voted no.

    Source: Senate roll call 314

Roger Marshall: the short answers

Did Roger Marshall sue his patients?

Yes. Marshall and his Great Bend OB-GYN practice filed debt-collection lawsuits against more than 700 patients from 1998 to 2019, over balances as small as $101, at 18 percent annual interest.

Source: The New York Times, from Kansas court records

Why were Roger Marshall's patients arrested?

Eighty-one of the patients he sued were arrested after missing court dates in those debt cases. His spokesperson says judges, not doctors, issue the warrants.

Source: The New York Times; KAKE News

How did Roger Marshall vote on health care?

He voted for the 2025 megabill and its Medicaid cuts, against a $35 insulin cap for private insurance in 2022, against extending ACA premium tax credits in 2025 and for the 2017 House bill to repeal the ACA.

Source: His votes, with roll calls

Does Roger Marshall live in Kansas?

He lists a cabin near St. John, appraised at $124,200, as his home. A trust bought a Sarasota, Fla., house in 2019 that was later valued at $1.2 million.

Source: Topeka Capital-Journal

Who is running against Roger Marshall?

Democrat Adam Hamilton, a United Methodist pastor. The general election is Nov. 3, 2026.

Source: Kansas Reflector, Aug. 4, 2026

How to vote in the Kansas Senate race

Kansas has not sent a Democrat to the U.S. Senate in more than 90 years. This race is close. Every date below comes from the Kansas secretary of state or state law.

  • Register by Oct. 13 Online with a Kansas driver's license or ID at the state registration page, or on the paper form. Moved or changed your name? Update it by the same date.
  • Check your registration Confirm you are registered, see your sample ballot and find your polling place at VoterView.
  • Vote early: Oct. 14 to Nov. 2 In-person advance voting starts as early as Oct. 14 and ends at noon Nov. 2. Days and hours vary by county; ask your county election office.
  • Vote by mail: apply by Oct. 27 Send the advance ballot application to your county (K.S.A. 25-1122). Your ballot must arrive by 7 p.m. Nov. 3. Drop it at your county election office, a county drop box or any polling place in your county.
  • Election Day: 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Some counties open earlier or close later. If you are in line when polls close, you can still vote.
  • Bring a photo ID A driver's license from any state, a passport, a military, tribal or Kansas student ID and others count. See the full list, including the free state ID.

Sources: Kansas secretary of state, important election dates; voter information; K.S.A. 25-1132; on the 90-year streak, CBS News.
